# Lightning Talk (5-10 Minutes)

## The Constraint = The Feature

Lightning talks are NOT shortened long talks. They're a different art form:
- Every second counts — no warm-up, no "about me", no filler
- One idea, delivered with impact
- Leave the audience wanting MORE, not less

## The Formula

**1 talk = 1 idea = 1 takeaway**

If you can't summarize your talk in one sentence, it's too broad for lightning format.

## Structure (for a 5-min talk)

| Time | Section | Purpose |
|------|---------|---------|
| 0:00-0:30 | Hook | Grab attention immediately |
| 0:30-1:00 | Context | Why this matters (30 sec max) |
| 1:00-4:00 | Core content | The one idea, with proof/demo |
| 4:00-4:30 | Takeaway | What to remember |
| 4:30-5:00 | CTA + close | What to do next |

For a 10-min talk: expand "Core content" to 7 min with 2-3 sub-points.

## Planning Process

1. **Write the one-sentence takeaway** FIRST
2. **Find the hook** — what's surprising, provocative, or relatable?
3. **Build the minimal proof** — one demo, one example, one story
4. **Cut everything else** — ruthlessly
5. **Rehearse to time** — you WILL run over without practice

## Best Practices

### Content
- **Start with the punchline** — reveal the conclusion early, then explain why
- **One story OR one demo OR one data point** — not all three
- **No agenda slides** — you have 5 minutes, not 45
- **Skip "about me"** — unless your background IS the point
- **End with a memorable phrase** — something tweetable
- **Include one visual** that sticks in memory

### Delivery
- **Memorize the opening** — don't fumble the first 10 seconds
- **No live demos** unless they're under 60 seconds with zero setup
- **Slides: max 10** for 5 min, **max 15** for 10 min
- **Speak faster than normal** — but pause for emphasis on key moments
- **No Q&A** — "find me after for questions"
- **Rehearse 5+ times** — short talks need MORE practice, not less

### Slide Design for Lightning
- Full-bleed images with 2-3 words overlaid
- One number or stat per slide (massive font)
- Code snippets: max 5 lines, syntax highlighted, 28pt+
- No bullet lists — they're for longer talks
- Dark backgrounds, high contrast text

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Trying to fit a 45-min talk into 5 minutes (death by compression)
- ❌ Starting with "so, um, my name is..." (wasted 10 seconds)
- ❌ Too many slides (clicking through rapid-fire is disorienting)
- ❌ Live demo with setup time (fatal in 5 min)
- ❌ No rehearsal ("it's just 5 minutes, how hard can it be?") — VERY hard
- ❌ Running over time (disrespectful to next speaker and audience)
- ❌ Ending with "and there's so much more I wanted to cover..." (anti-climax)
- ❌ Q&A attempts ("we don't really have time but any quick questions?")

## Checklist

### Preparation
- [ ] One-sentence takeaway written
- [ ] Hook that works in < 10 seconds
- [ ] Core content: ONE idea with ONE proof point
- [ ] Slides: under 10 (for 5 min) or 15 (for 10 min)
- [ ] No "about me" slide (unless essential)
- [ ] Memorable closing line
- [ ] CTA (URL, QR code, or next step)

### Rehearsal
- [ ] Timed rehearsal #1 (expect to run over)
- [ ] Cut content to fit (remove, don't speed up)
- [ ] Timed rehearsal #2 (should be close)
- [ ] Timed rehearsal #3 (nail the timing)
- [ ] Record yourself — watch for filler words and pacing
- [ ] Practice transitions (no "um, next slide...")

### Day Of
- [ ] Slides loaded and tested (don't rely on your machine connecting)
- [ ] Backup: PDF on USB drive
- [ ] Timer visible or trusted timekeeper in audience
- [ ] Opening line memorized
- [ ] Water sip BEFORE you start (not during 5 min)

## Inspiration Formats

### "One Weird Trick"
- "I found something nobody talks about. Here's what it is and why it matters."
- Works for: surprising findings, counter-intuitive practices

### "Before/After"
- "Here's how we used to do X. Here's what changed. Here's the result."
- Works for: tool recommendations, process improvements

### "Live Build"
- "Watch me build X in 4 minutes. Zero to working."
- Works for: developer tools, frameworks, APIs
- ⚠️ High risk — must be perfectly rehearsed

### "The Rant"
- "This thing is broken and here's why."
- Works for: controversial opinions, calling out bad practices
- End with a positive alternative

### "Show & Tell"
- "I built this thing. Let me show you what it does."
- Works for: side projects, open source, internal tools
