# Live Presentation (1:M Physical)

## Scope

Any in-person presentation where one speaker (or co-speakers) presents to a seated audience:
- Conference breakout sessions (AWS Summit, re:Invent, community days)
- Internal knowledge-sharing (Floor 28, brown bags, tech talks)
- Customer-facing briefings and demos
- Meetup and community talks
- All-hands and town halls

## Planning Process

1. **Define the session** — topic, audience level (L100-L400), duration, venue constraints
2. **Research** — use `deep_research` for content depth, competitor talks, audience expectations
3. **Structure** — build the narrative arc
4. **Create deliverables** — slide outline, speaker notes, demo plan

## Session Structure Template

### Opening (10-15% of time)
- **Hook** — surprising stat, provocative question, or relatable pain point
- **Credibility** — brief intro establishing why you're the right speaker
- **Promise** — what the audience will walk away with (be specific)
- **Agenda preview** — 3-4 bullet road map

### Body (70-80% of time)
- **3-5 key points** — each with its own mini-arc:
  - Assertion → Evidence → Example → Takeaway
- **Progressive disclosure** — start broad, go deep
- **Pattern interrupts** every 7-10 minutes:
  - Live demo or code walkthrough
  - Audience poll or question
  - Story or anecdote
  - Shift from slides to whiteboard
- **Transitions** — explicit bridges between sections

### Closing (10-15% of time)
- **Recap** — 3 bullet summary of key takeaways
- **Call to action** — what should they do next?
- **Resources** — QR code to slides, repo, docs
- **Q&A** — leave 5-10 min

## Best Practices

### Content Design
- One idea per slide — if you need a "build" use animation or multiple slides
- Maximum 3 bullet points per slide; prefer visuals/diagrams
- Every slide must pass the "so what?" test — why does the audience care?
- Include a "why should I care?" moment in the first 2 minutes
- L200+ sessions: lead with the architecture diagram, then drill down
- Never read your slides — they're visual aids, not a script

### Delivery
- Rehearse out loud at least 3 times (not just in your head)
- Time yourself — cut 10% to leave breathing room
- Arrive early — test A/V, walk the stage, feel the room
- Make eye contact with different sections of the room
- Pause after key points (3 seconds feels long, but works)
- Move with purpose — don't pace, but don't stand frozen either

### Audience Engagement
- Ask a question in the first 5 minutes ("raise your hand if...")
- Use live polls (Slido, Mentimeter) for large audiences
- Build in "turn to your neighbor" moments for sessions > 30 min
- Acknowledge expertise in the room ("some of you probably know more about X")
- Have a backup plan if demos fail

### Technical Talks Specifically
- Show the demo first, explain the theory second (invert the textbook)
- Use real-world scale numbers, not toy examples
- Include a "what can go wrong" section — audiences love war stories
- Link to code repos — attendees want to reproduce what you showed
- Architecture diagrams: start with the 10,000ft view, then zoom in

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Too many slides (>1 per minute of talk time)
- ❌ Starting with "about me" for more than 30 seconds
- ❌ No clear takeaway — audience leaves unsure what they learned
- ❌ Reading from slides or notes
- ❌ Skipping rehearsal — "I'll just wing it"
- ❌ Demo-heavy without backup screenshots/video
- ❌ Ending with "any questions?" to silence (prime Q&A with a planted question)
- ❌ Running over time — shows disrespect for audience
- ❌ Assuming homogeneous audience level

## Checklist

### 2 Weeks Before
- [ ] Session structure finalized
- [ ] Key messages defined (max 3)
- [ ] Slides in draft
- [ ] Demo environment prepared
- [ ] Backup plan for demo failures (screenshots/video)

### 1 Week Before
- [ ] Full rehearsal (timed)
- [ ] Slides reviewed by a peer
- [ ] Speaker notes complete
- [ ] QR code/short URL for resources
- [ ] Tech check scheduled (A/V, screen resolution, clicker)

### Day Of
- [ ] Arrive 30 min early
- [ ] Test display, audio, clicker, internet
- [ ] Water bottle ready
- [ ] Phone on silent
- [ ] Opening line memorized (don't fumble the start)
- [ ] Backup slides on USB drive

### After
- [ ] Share slides/recording within 24 hours
- [ ] Collect feedback (survey link or verbal)
- [ ] Note what worked and what didn't for next time

## AWS-Specific Tips

- For Summit/re:Invent: sessions are recorded — speak clearly for transcription
- AWS sessions use the "Builder" angle — always show how to DO it, not just what it IS
- Include a "get started in 5 minutes" moment
- Reference Well-Architected Framework pillars where relevant
- Floor 28 sessions: more informal, encourage interruptions, bring a live terminal
