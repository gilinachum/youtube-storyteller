# 1:1 Meeting Planning

## What a 1:1 Is (and Isn't)

A 1:1 is a **relationship and growth conversation**, not a status update. The manager's job is to listen, unblock, and develop — not to receive project reports (those belong in team syncs).

**The 1:1 belongs to the direct report.** They set the agenda. The manager shows up prepared to support.

## Cadence & Duration

| Relationship | Recommended Cadence | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| New employee (first 90 days) | Weekly | 45-60 min |
| Ongoing direct report | Bi-weekly | 30-45 min |
| Senior/experienced IC | Monthly | 45-60 min |
| Skip-level 1:1 | Quarterly | 30-45 min |
| Peer 1:1 | Monthly or as needed | 30 min |

Never skip more than two weeks in a row — trust erodes fast.

## Agenda Structure (Recommended)

### Opening (2-3 min)
- Quick personal check-in: "How are you doing — really?"
- Acknowledge anything significant since last 1:1 (a win, a hard week, a milestone)

### Direct Report's Topics (15-20 min)
- They go first — always
- Shared agenda doc in advance (Google Doc, Notion, etc.)
- Common topics employees bring:
  - Blockers and frustrations
  - Uncertainty or anxiety about their work
  - Interpersonal issues
  - Feedback on decisions or direction
  - Career and growth questions
  - Ideas they want to share

### Manager's Topics (5-10 min)
- Feedback (recognition or developmental — specific, timely)
- Context sharing (what's happening above/around them)
- Coaching on a challenge they raised
- One thing you need from them

### Closing (2-3 min)
- Summarize action items (who does what by when)
- Reaffirm anything sensitive discussed
- Preview next 1:1 topic if relevant

## Shared Agenda Doc

Maintain a rolling Google Doc or Notion page. Both parties add items before the meeting.

**Template:**
```
1:1 — [Name] + [Manager] | [Date]

Employee Topics:
- 

Manager Topics:
- 

Action Items from Last 1:1:
- [ ] 

Notes:
```

Review action items from last session at the start of every 1:1.

## Manager Listening Techniques

- **Ask, don't tell** — "What do you think is blocking this?" before offering answers
- **Name the emotion** — "Sounds like you're frustrated with the timeline, is that right?"
- **The 2-second pause** — after they finish, wait 2 seconds before responding; they often have more to say
- **Avoid advice-giving too fast** — diagnose before prescribing
- **Mirror language** — reflect back what you hear to confirm understanding

## Common 1:1 Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It's Harmful | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Running a status update | Employee can't raise real concerns | Ban project updates; redirect to async |
| Canceling frequently | Signals they're not a priority | Protect the calendar; reschedule don't cancel |
| Manager dominates | Employee doesn't get heard | Set a timer; manager talks < 40% |
| No agenda | Meeting wanders; wastes time | Shared doc, both add before meeting |
| No follow-through | Erodes trust fast | Track action items; review them first |
| Always surface-level | Misses real issues | Ask "What's really going on?" |

## Career & Growth Conversations

Reserve at least one 1:1 per quarter for a focused career conversation:

- **Where do you want to be in 2 years?** (role, skills, lifestyle)
- **What's working well that you want to do more of?**
- **What's draining you or slowing you down?**
- **What skills do you want to build this year?**
- **What does success look like for you in the next 6 months?**

Build a **growth plan** together: specific skills + concrete opportunities to practice them.

## Giving Feedback in a 1:1

Use **SBI (Situation → Behavior → Impact)**:
- **Situation:** "In yesterday's team meeting..."
- **Behavior:** "...you interrupted Maya twice while she was presenting..."
- **Impact:** "...I noticed she went quiet afterward and I'm concerned it affected her confidence."

Then: "I wanted to raise this directly. What's your read on what happened?"

**Recognition feedback** follows the same format — be specific, not generic:
- ❌ "Great job on the project."
- ✅ "In the client meeting on Thursday, you handled the pricing pushback really calmly and kept the conversation moving — that saved the deal."

## Hebrew Localization Notes

- Directness is valued in Israeli work culture — it's OK to get to the point quickly
- Hierarchical distance is lower than in many Western cultures — employees may challenge up more readily
- Be aware of holiday cycles (Chagim) affecting scheduling — 1:1s go on hold during Sukkot/Pesach/etc.
- Use "שיחת אחד על אחד" or just "וואן-און-וואן"
