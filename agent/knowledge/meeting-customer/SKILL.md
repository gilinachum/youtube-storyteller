# Customer Meeting Planning

## Types of Customer Meetings

Each type has a different primary goal and structure:

| Type | Primary Goal | Who Leads |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery Call | Understand the customer's problem and context | AE / CSM |
| Demo / Presentation | Show how your solution solves their problem | AE / SE |
| Business Review (QBR/EBR) | Prove value delivered; renew/expand | CSM / Account Lead |
| Onboarding Kickoff | Set up for success; define expectations | CSM / Solutions |
| Renewal / Expansion | Confirm continued value; close next deal | CSM / AE |
| Escalation / Recovery | Rebuild trust after a problem | CSM + Leadership |

---

## Discovery Call

### Goal
Understand the customer's situation well enough to know: (1) do we have a fit? (2) what does a solution look like for them?

### Structure (45-60 min)
1. **Rapport (3 min)** — personal opener; acknowledge any context you know
2. **Agenda setting (2 min)** — "Here's what I hope we'll cover; does that work for you?"
3. **Situation (10 min)** — Current state; what they're doing today; team/process context
4. **Problem (10 min)** — What's not working; what's the pain; how long has this been a problem
5. **Impact (5 min)** — What does this problem cost them (time, money, risk, opportunity)
6. **Need / Vision (5 min)** — What does "solved" look like? What would they do if it were fixed?
7. **Exploration (10 min)** — Share 2-3 relevant insights or questions; do NOT pitch yet
8. **Next steps (5 min)** — Clear, specific, mutually agreed next action

### SPIN Questions (Use Liberally)
- **Situation:** "Walk me through how your team currently handles X?"
- **Problem:** "What are the biggest frustrations with that process?"
- **Implication:** "When that breaks down, what happens downstream?"
- **Need-Payoff:** "If you could fix that, what would it enable for the business?"

### The #1 Rule
Listen 70%, talk 30%. Your job in discovery is to understand, not to pitch.

## Demo / Solution Presentation

### Goal
Show how your solution solves THEIR specific problems (not a generic feature tour).

### Structure (45-60 min)
1. **Re-confirm their pain (5 min)** — "Based on our last conversation, the key challenges were X and Y — is that still right?"
2. **Framing (3 min)** — "I'm going to focus today on how we solve exactly those two things"
3. **Demo (25-30 min)** — Problem → solution flow; use their language; show the outcome, not features
4. **Proof (5 min)** — Reference customer, case study, or metric that mirrors their situation
5. **Q&A (10 min)**
6. **Next steps (5 min)**

### Demo Best Practices
- Use a **sandbox environment** with their company name / realistic data
- Show **the outcome** first ("Here's what this looks like when it works"), then how to get there
- Don't demonstrate features nobody asked about — it adds noise
- Pause frequently: "Does this map to what you're dealing with?"
- Avoid speaking to the slides; speak to the customer

## Quarterly / Executive Business Review (QBR / EBR)

### Goal
Demonstrate the value you've delivered; align on future roadmap; secure renewal or expansion.

### Structure (60-90 min)
1. **Executive opening (5 min)** — Restate business goals they told you 90 days ago
2. **Value delivered (20 min)** — Usage, outcomes, ROI — in their language (not your metrics)
3. **Challenges / What didn't work (5 min)** — Be honest; shows maturity and trust
4. **Customer health (5 min)** — NPS / satisfaction / adoption trends
5. **Roadmap preview (10 min)** — What's coming that's relevant to them
6. **Goals for next quarter (10 min)** — Mutually define success for the next 90 days
7. **Commercial discussion (10 min)** — Renewal / expansion if applicable
8. **Open Q&A (15 min)**

### EBR Prep (1 week before)
- Pull actual usage and outcome data — tie to their stated goals
- Prep the 3 hardest questions they might ask and your answers
- Confirm the right attendees (executive sponsor must be there)
- Send pre-read 48 hours in advance

## Meeting Preparation Checklist (All Types)

### Research (Before Every Meeting)
- [ ] Review last meeting notes and action items
- [ ] Check their LinkedIn / news for context (funding, headcount, leadership changes)
- [ ] Review their usage data / support tickets if available
- [ ] Know their industry and main business challenge

### Agenda
- [ ] Send agenda 24 hours before — with stated goal of the meeting
- [ ] Confirm attendees and their roles
- [ ] Identify the economic buyer, champion, and skeptic in the room

### Technology
- [ ] Confirm video link / room setup
- [ ] Test screen share / demo environment
- [ ] Have a backup plan if tech fails (local slides, PDF backup)

## During the Meeting

### Opening
- Thank them for their time — genuinely
- State the goal: "By the end of today I want us to have X"
- Confirm: "Does the agenda still work, or is there something urgent to add?"

### Active Listening Cues
- Take visible notes — it signals respect and you'll remember more
- Repeat back what you heard: "So what I'm understanding is..."
- Don't interrupt — let them finish even if you know where they're going

### Handling Objections
- Acknowledge first: "That's a fair concern"
- Clarify before responding: "Can you tell me more about what's driving that?"
- Respond to the root concern, not the surface objection

### Reading the Room
- Quiet executive = either bored or thinking — ask: "What's your reaction so far?"
- Side conversations = they're interested but need to discuss — pause and invite them in
- Clock-watching = time is being wasted — accelerate to the point

---

## Closing the Meeting

**Always end with explicit next steps:**
- What will YOU do, by when?
- What will THEY do, by when?
- When is the next touchpoint?

**Weak close:** "Great meeting! I'll follow up."
**Strong close:** "So agreed: I'll send you the proposal by Thursday. You'll share it with your CFO by Friday. We'll connect on Monday at 2pm to discuss. Does that work?"

---

## Post-Meeting Follow-Up

Send within 2 hours:
- Summary of what was discussed (3-5 bullets)
- Action items with owners and dates
- Any materials promised
- Confirm the next meeting

**Template:**
```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the time today. Here's a quick recap:

Key takeaways:
• 

Action items:
• [You] — [action] by [date]
• [Them] — [action] by [date]

Next step: [meeting / call / decision] on [date/time]

Let me know if I missed anything.

[Signature]
```

## Hebrew / Israeli Customer Context

- Israeli decision-making can be fast and informal — "החלטנו" in a hallway conversation is real
- Hierarchy matters less in Israeli companies; ICs may have strong influence on decisions
- Directness is normal — "זה לא עובד לנו" is feedback, not rejection
- Relationships are key — small talk and personal connection are not wasted time
- WhatsApp is often the primary business communication channel for Israeli customers
