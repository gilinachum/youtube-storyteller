# All-Hands / Town Hall Meeting Planning

## What an All-Hands Is For

An all-hands is a **leadership communication event**. Its purpose is to:
- Build alignment on where the company/team is going
- Reinforce culture and shared purpose
- Give employees direct access to leadership
- Address uncertainty or hard questions honestly
- Celebrate wins and people

**What it is NOT:** A collection of department status updates read off a slide.

## Audience & Scale

| Scale | Format Recommendation |
|---|---|
| < 50 people | Single room or video call; high interactivity possible |
| 50-200 people | Auditorium or large video call; moderate interactivity |
| 200-500 people | Video production quality needed; structured Q&A |
| 500+ people | Broadcast model; pre-screened Q&A; breakout by team after |

## Recommended Cadence

| Frequency | Best For |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Fast-moving startups, high uncertainty periods |
| Quarterly | Most companies — aligns with business cadence |
| Bi-annual | Large orgs; supplement with team-level all-hands |

## Agenda Template (60-minute format)

### 1. Opening — CEO/Leader (5 min)
- Personal tone, not formal
- Acknowledge what the company has been through recently (hard or good)
- Set the theme/narrative for today's meeting
- **One question to anchor on:** "What do we most need the team to understand after this meeting?"

### 2. Company Pulse — Where We Stand (15 min)
- Key metrics: What are they, where were we 90 days ago, where are we now
- Be honest — good and bad. Trust is built by not spinning.
- 3 wins since last all-hands (specific, named)
- 1-2 challenges acknowledged directly
- **Avoid:** Death by data. Pick 3-5 metrics maximum. Use visuals.

### 3. Strategic Focus — Where We're Going (15 min)
- What are the top priorities for the next quarter?
- What's changing? What's NOT changing?
- How does each team connect to these priorities?
- If there's been a pivot or a hard decision — explain the "why" directly

### 4. Spotlight / Recognition (5 min)
- Name specific individuals or teams who went above and beyond
- Be concrete: "The infra team kept us live during the AWS outage on May 3rd with zero customer impact — that was exceptional."
- Avoid generic praise; it dilutes the signal

### 5. Open Q&A (15 min)
- This is the most valuable part — don't cut it
- Use a moderated format: submissions via Slido, anonymous option
- Answer hard questions directly; "I don't know but I'll find out" is a valid and trusted answer
- Avoid: filtering out critical questions — employees notice and trust drops

### 6. Close (5 min)
- Re-anchor on the 1-2 things you want people to leave remembering
- Call to action: what do you want employees to DO differently after this?
- Personal close — something human, not corporate

## Content Preparation

### 4 Weeks Before
- Align leadership on key messages
- Decide what metrics/data to share
- Collect spotlight nominations
- Build slide structure (no content yet)

### 2 Weeks Before
- Draft slides; review with leadership
- Prepare Q&A simulation: list the 10 hardest questions employees will ask and draft honest answers
- Schedule dry run

### 1 Week Before
- Full dry run with all presenters — time it
- Open Q&A submission (Slido, anonymous form) in advance so real questions surface
- Confirm AV / hybrid setup

### Day Before
- Final slide review
- Brief all presenters on timing and handoffs
- Test tech setup end-to-end

## Slide Design Principles

- **One idea per slide** — not a wall of text
- **Data with narrative** — show the number, but say what it means
- **Photos of people** — employees want to see themselves and colleagues
- **No jargon** — plain language; if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough
- **Slide count:** For a 60-min all-hands, aim for 20-30 slides maximum

## Q&A Best Practices

### Collecting Questions
- Use **Slido**, **Mentimeter**, or **Google Forms** — anonymous submissions increase honesty
- Open submissions 48 hours in advance for thoughtful questions
- Collect live questions during the meeting for real-time topics

### Answering
- Don't deflect hard questions — paraphrase and address directly
- "I don't know" + follow-up commitment is more trusted than a non-answer
- If the question is common: "I see a lot of versions of this question — here's what I'm hearing..."
- Assign a follow-up owner when the answer needs more research

### Moderation
- Assign a moderator (not the CEO) to manage flow
- Group similar questions
- Reserve right to combine/reframe: "This is a version of..."
- Don't read the question robotically — internalize it and respond naturally

## Hybrid All-Hands

- Use a camera operator so remote viewers see speaker, not just slides
- Run a separate chat moderator for remote Q&A
- Display chat questions alongside in-room questions — treat them equally
- Record and share within 24 hours for those who couldn't attend live

## Common All-Hands Mistakes

| Mistake | Impact | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pure status update format | Employees feel like they got a report, not a conversation | Lead with narrative, not metrics |
| No hard topics addressed | Erodes trust — employees know what's happening | Name the elephant in the room |
| No Q&A or filtered Q&A | Signals leadership doesn't want accountability | Open, anonymous, unfiltered |
| Too long (>90 min) | Attention collapses; message is lost | Hard cap at 75 min; cut content, not Q&A |
| Generic recognition | Feels performative | Specific names + specific actions |
| No follow-through on Q&A commitments | Trust killer | Assign owners; publish answers async |

## Post-Meeting

- Publish the recording and slide deck within 24 hours
- Publish written answers to questions that came in but weren't addressed live
- Manager cascade: ask team leads to hold a quick team debrief to reinforce the messages
- Survey: simple 2-question pulse ("What resonated? What's unclear?")
